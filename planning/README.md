# Planning

These three files are what I wrote **before** I built anything.

They no longer describe the site. I kept them anyway, because the gap between
them and what shipped is more interesting than either one on its own.

| File | What it was |
|---|---|
| [story.md](story.md) | The narrative in eight acts, written first, before any HTML |
| [sections.md](sections.md) | What each section had to *do* and how it should *feel* |
| [design.md](design.md) | The same, compressed to one goal and one feeling per section |

---

## What changed between the plan and the ship

**Eight acts became twelve chapters.** The plan had one section for "the
projects." Once I wrote them out, each project had a different lesson worth
its own space, so it split.

**Two chapters exist that I never planned.** *The receipts* and *The honest
part* both came from re-reading the job posting after the site already
existed. The posting asks for three pull requests on day one, and I had shipped
six without mentioning it anywhere. It also asks for strong React and
TypeScript, which I do not have, and the first draft danced around that instead
of saying it.

**One chapter I planned took the longest to earn.** *One idea* was in the plan
from the start as "Product Idea." I did not have anything worth putting there
until the failures chapter was written. The idea came out of those failures.

**"Interactive timeline" and "clickable screenshots" did not happen.** They are
in `sections.md`. They were features looking for a reason. The build log that
replaced them is more useful and took less time.

**The tone got more direct.** The plan said "honest." The first draft was
polite. Compare `story.md` Act VI to chapter eleven on the site — the
substance is the same, but the plan hedges and the site does not.

---

## Why these are still here

The plan being wrong is not the embarrassing part. Shipping something that
contradicts your own documentation and not noticing is.

If I had deleted these, the repository would look tidier and tell you less.
